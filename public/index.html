How Aimless works

A walk, authored by chance. Press Go and Aimless draws a chain of waypoints you can't see on a map - only an arrow and a distance. At each waypoint the voice you chose puts a thought in your head. You take a photo if you want one. When you come back you hold a small artifact: the shape you walked, the photos, the words. A machine for serendipity, really. You supply the wandering, it supplies the accident.

Starting

Choose your settings, face the direction you'd like to drift, and press Go: the first waypoint lands roughly ahead of you.

During

Follow the arrow. Within about 30 metres of a waypoint the card is revealed and you're asked for a photo. The circle is generous on purpose: phone GPS drifts, so an exact spot could never be reached. If a waypoint is unreachable, tap "Close as I can get" and the walk accepts where you stood.

After

You get a self-contained HTML artifact of your walk that you can download or share: your route, your photos + the waypoint 'thoughts' that your coordinates generated. The My Voice option contains no extra text and an option to add your own writing. Open a walk from the Archive and add styling: choose a skin and apply filters to the images: long-press to select an image, again to deselect.

Sharing

A walk can leave your phone three ways: the Card, a square image of the route over your featured photo; the HTML file, one self-contained document with no scripts, so it opens cleanly on iOS and inside WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Discord or email; and a live page on your Neocities site, usually the best option for Facebook and anywhere that renders a link preview.

Offline and privacy

Everything runs on your device and works offline once loaded. Nothing is uploaded. A walk leaves the phone only as a file you choose to export, so export the ones you want to keep. Installing to the home screen gives the storage the best protection and gives a full-screen experience.

FAQ

Which browser should I use?

Android: Chrome. It installs Aimless as a real app: its own icon, its own window, protected storage. Most other Android browsers only pin a shortcut, and some restrict the sensors Aimless needs.

iPhone: Safari. iOS only lets Safari install to the home screen.

How do I install it?

Chrome on Android: tap the ⋮ menu, then "Add to Home screen" or "Install app". Safari on iPhone: tap Share, then "Add to Home Screen". After that, always open Aimless from its icon.

Which permissions matter?

Location (and motion, on iPhone): both are essential, both are asked for on first open. If you denied one:

  • Chrome: tap the icon left of the address bar, then Permissions.
  • iPhone: iOS won't ask twice. Remove the app, re-add it from Safari, and check Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services.
  • Brave: it blocks motion sensors by default, so the compass never moves. Allow them under brave://settings/content/sensors. And "Clear browsing data on exit" wipes your saved walks: turn it off, or use Chrome.
Will my walks survive?

Only if you export them. Walks live in browser storage, which browsers can clear under pressure or via privacy settings. Open a walk from the Archive and tap export: the HTML file is self-contained, opens anywhere, and needs no app. Installing Aimless to the home screen gives storage the best protection. For the whole collection at once, Back up on the Archive screen writes walks, photos and saved filters to one JSON file; Restore brings them back, which is also how everything moves to a new phone.

What are the voices?
  • The Crow - the appraising eye. Knows the difference between a thing that has been dropped and a thing that has been left.
  • The Threshold - the place that is neither. Doorways, crossings, and the moment in between.
  • The Lattice - the grid that does not fit. Imposes its own order on whatever it finds.
  • The Inner - the oracle. It reads places, not people.
  • The Stray - the voice of what wanders. Animals, ghosts, memories, and the parts of you that refuse to stay put.
  • The Small - the world under your feet. A whole landscape in every crack.
  • The Slow - the thing with more time than you. Everything is moving; it is simply moving slower than you are.
  • The Echo - the one that repeats you. The spot answers with a version of what it always says. You happen to be standing in it.
  • My Voice - your own words, or none. No cards on the walk; attach a text file at export and each block of it lands beneath a photo.

Pause and ponder the lines you are given. Sometimes they turn out to have been waiting for you.

What if a waypoint is somewhere I shouldn't go?

Then don't go. Waypoints are random points; they know nothing of fences, railways, water, or private land. Use common sense, respect private property, and when in doubt tap "Close as I can get": the walk accepts where you stood and moves on.

What does the Surprise filter do?

It doesn't pick a filter, it rolls one: a random stack of effects stored on that photo. Most rolls are ordinary, occasionally one is golden. Keep tapping until one clicks, then leave it alone: the next tap replaces it. A roll worth keeping: tap Save. in the rail, name it, and it joins your filters like any other. It works on exactly one selected photo at a time.

punk mode

Use Aimless in punk mode: start a walk but don't follow the arrow. Capture a series of 3, 5 or 7 photos on the way: when you get to a good spot tap "Close as I can get", take a photo, OK, Continue. It's fine to turn your screen off between photos, but the GPS trace is only recorded while the app is open.

What are the KML options?

KML is the route format mapping apps understand. "Export Route" downloads a .kml; "Add KML link to footer" embeds the route inside the HTML artifact. Use the link knowingly: it contains the walk's real GPS coordinates, so if you started from your front door, anyone holding the file can find it.

What is Wobbletone?

Wobbletone is an image effects playground that is easy to use for infinite creative possibilities. You build a stack of effects, each with its own settings; these are the same format as Aimless filters. Create a set of amazing custom filters and send them to Aimless: each preset's Export button saves exactly the file the Filters sheet imports.

How do I import my own filters?

Tap the + at the end of the Filter rail on a walk's detail page, then import one filter at a time: paste the code from Wobbletone's Code tab, or tap Export on a Wobbletone preset to save a .json file and bring it in with choose file. Get Wobbletone at wobbletone.com.

How do I tidy the filter rail?

The same + opens the Filters sheet, where every filter shows a live preview on one of the walk's photos. Any filter can be hidden so the rail stays short. Your own filters, saved or imported, can also be exported as .json files to share, or deleted.

Can I put a walk on my Neocities site?

Yes. Open a walk from the Archive, tap export, then Neocities page. Aimless asks for your site name once, then builds the files for you: the page, a preview image, and an index page that lists your published walks. Upload them to a folder called aimless on your site, and the page is live with a proper preview when you share the link.

Anyone with the link can see the page, so publish the walks you want seen. Your Neocities password and API key are never asked for.

The preview only knows where your page lives because of the folder: keep the page and its image together in aimless, or the link preview loses its image.

Neocities serves index.html at /aimless/: a ready-made front page for your walks. Link to it from your homepage, or upload it as your homepage if the site is walks only. A walk joins the list when you tick the index checkbox at build time, or later on the published step; Update index makes a fresh index.html to upload after you change your mind.

Want a page of your own design instead? Copy file list puts your published walks on the clipboard as plain text: paths, titles, dates, descriptions. Feed it to an LLM with a creative prompt, or build the page by hand. Aimless only knows about walks published from this device.

Why is there no map?

The absence is the design. Aimless is about being where you are, not navigating somewhere. You follow an arrow, not a route, and the shape of your walk is only revealed afterwards.
